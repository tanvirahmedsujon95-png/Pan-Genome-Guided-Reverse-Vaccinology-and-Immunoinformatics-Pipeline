# In Silico Multi-Epitope Vaccine Design Against *Providencia stuartii*

## Overview
This repository presents the computational workflow used to design and evaluate a multi-epitope vaccine candidate against *Providencia stuartii* using reverse vaccinology and immunoinformatics approaches.

## Objectives
- Identify potential vaccine candidate proteins through pan-genome analysis.
- Predict and screen B-cell and T-cell epitopes.
- Construct a multi-epitope vaccine using selected epitopes, linkers, and an adjuvant.
- Evaluate the construct through structural analysis, immune simulation, and molecular docking.
- Assess molecular stability using molecular dynamics simulations.
- Perform codon optimization for potential recombinant expression.

## Methodology
The workflow includes:

1. Genome retrieval and pan-genome analysis.
2. Antigen screening and prioritization.
3. B-cell and T-cell epitope prediction.
4. Population coverage analysis.
5. Multi-epitope vaccine construction.
6. Physicochemical and structural characterization.
7. Immune simulation.
8. Molecular docking and interaction analysis.
9. Molecular dynamics simulation and binding-energy analysis.
10. Codon optimization and in silico cloning.

## Tools and Resources
The study used several bioinformatics tools and platforms, including:

- NCBI Datasets, Prokka, and Roary
- VaxiJen, PSORTb, SignalP, and AllerTOP
- IEDB, NetMHCpan, and NetMHCIIpan
- ExPASy ProtParam and SOLpro
- AlphaFold 3 and GalaxyRefine
- C-ImmSim and ClusPro
- PRODIGY and GROMACS
- JCat and SnapGene

## Repository Contents
This repository contains the methodology tutorial, relevant computational resources, and supporting materials associated with the study. Additional input files, output files, and analysis scripts may be added as they become available.

## Limitations
All vaccine properties and biological interactions evaluated in this study are computational predictions. Experimental validation is necessary to establish immunogenicity, safety, and protective efficacy.
